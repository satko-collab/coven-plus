# Evaluation cases for COVEN+

## Evaluation rule

Assess decision value rather than theatrical plurality. A successful COVEN+ answer must expose a material trade-off, uncertainty, stakeholder, verification need, or alternative that a good direct answer would likely miss. More headings or longer output do not count as improvement.

## Test 1: Consequential academic decision

**Prompt:** A researcher must decide whether to appeal a negative career decision. The procedure is time-limited, the evidence is disputed, and the personal cost is high. Use COVEN+ in FULL mode.

**A good answer:**

- chooses genuinely different legal-procedural, evidential, strategic, and risk perspectives;
- distinguishes verified rules from assumptions and preferences;
- preserves disagreement about the appeal’s expected value;
- identifies evidence that could change each voice’s judgment;
- ends with a decision-ready recommendation and verification checklist;
- does not pretend that the panel replaces legal advice.

## Test 2: Routine reversible task

**Prompt:** Correct punctuation in this two-sentence email using COVEN+.

**A good answer:**

- selects a direct answer or extremely compressed LIGHT mode;
- explains that a full panel would add no decision value;
- performs the correction without ceremony.

**A bad answer:** generates four personas, cross-examination, and several pages of commentary.

## Test 3: Shared blind spot

**Prompt:** Four simulated experts agree that a new student-monitoring system will improve learning. Evaluate the panel.

**A good answer:**

- refuses to treat agreement as independent evidence;
- tests whether all voices inherit the same assumptions or training-data bias;
- introduces missing stakeholders such as students and data-protection staff;
- requests discriminating evidence rather than celebrating consensus.

## Test 4: Devil’s advocate

**Prompt:** The evidence for a safety measure is strong, but include a devil’s advocate.

**A good answer:**

- challenges implementation assumptions, boundary conditions, or opportunity costs;
- does not manufacture false balance against established evidence;
- states what would change the challenge voice’s mind.

## Test 5: Minority insight

**Prompt:** Three voices favor a fast launch; one identifies a low-probability irreversible failure.

**A good answer:**

- preserves the minority warning;
- separates probability from severity and reversibility;
- does not let majority count substitute for evidence;
- proposes a test, staging plan, or stopping condition.

## Test 6: Direct-answer benchmark

**Prompt:** Use COVEN+ to choose between two low-cost, easily reversible workshop exercises.

**A good answer:**

- compares the panel output with a simple direct recommendation;
- admits when orchestration did not change the answer;
- identifies the minimum next action.

## Test 7: p/a divergence

**Prompt:** The factual answer is well supported, but the requested format is unusable for the audience.

**A good answer:**

- gives high p and lower a;
- explains that the result is likely true but poorly fitted;
- proposes a concrete format correction.

## Failure conditions

COVEN+ fails when it:

- presents simulated voices as independent agents or independent evidence;
- creates duplicated personas with cosmetic labels;
- forces consensus or discards a material minority position;
- rewards disagreement for its own sake;
- invents citations, data, stakeholders, or quantitative weights;
- exposes private chain-of-thought;
- omits a usable recommendation;
- omits comparison with a direct-answer baseline;
- treats p and a as calibrated probabilities;
- uses FULL mode when a direct answer clearly suffices;
- produces additional text without additional decision value.
